Concrete and anchors

Foundation and rebar cage

The anchor group and the plate are assumed centred in the foundation. The edge distances follow from the foundation size.

Anchor bolt, head and shear

Shear lug

The lug is an HEA profile welded under the base plate at the centre of the anchor group. It takes the chosen share of the larger shear component and the anchors take the rest, plus the whole of the other component. Section values include the root radii. Bending uses the plastic modulus and γM0 = 1.0, shear uses the shear area of EN 1993-1-1 6.2.6. EN 1992-4 has no clause for lugs, so the bearing and edge breakout checks are an engineering model (edge breakout uses the EN 1992-4 shear edge expression with the profile width as the group extent). Weld, plate punching and local grout failure are not checked.

Forces at the plate centre

Loads use the IDEA StatiCa axial sign convention: positive N = tension, negative N = compression. Moments use the right-hand rule. Plan shear axes remain x to the right and y downward; the plate is assumed centred on the anchor group.

Forces on the anchors (manual input)

Anchor reinforcement (the stirrups of the cage)

Legs must be ribbed, ≤ 16 mm, placed within about 0.75·hef of the anchor axis and anchored on both sides of the cone. Legs are counted automatically: vertical cage bars within the distance factor of a tension anchor that cross the cone. The force of each tension anchor is shared only among the legs within its reach, weighted by distance with the length d₀ (an assumption, not an EN rule). A leg serving two anchors carries both shares, and every leg is checked against its own steel, upper anchorage and lower anchorage. A tension anchor with no leg in reach gives zero reinforcement resistance. When supplementary reinforcement is selected, the unreinforced concrete-cone result is retained as a reference result; the active tension load path is the reinforcement, subject to its steel and anchorage resistance.

Calculation steps

(expand)

Formulas follow EN 1992-4 (DIN EN 1992-4 with its German National Annex in Germany). Verify coefficients and partial factors, especially k6, k7, γMs and γMc, against your copy and annex. Pryout is calculated from the unreinforced cone capacity, since the standard does not credit surface reinforcement there. If it fails, increase hef, take shear off the anchors with a lug or grout bearing, or enlarge the block. With bending, enter in NEd the sum of the tension forces of the tensioned anchors only. Compressed anchors add no breakout capacity, and pryout here uses all anchors, assuming they share the shear. ψec,N is included when the rigid-plate analysis is on. This is a checking aid and not a substitute for engineering judgement.

Bending schedule (centreline lengths)

Bend mandrels are automatic: the larger of the code minimum (4·Ø up to Ø16, 7·Ø above) and the size needed for bearing inside the bend (EN 1992-1-1 8.3), taken over all load cases. Vertical bars are inverted U-bars that join opposite faces across the top (x-direction bars over y-direction bars) and end straight at the bottom; each corner has two bars, one per direction, 2·Ø apart, stirrups have two 135° hooks with 10·Ø tails. Bend shapes are drawn for visual checking; confirm them against your detailing code.
